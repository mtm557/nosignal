Your warranty survives the change

Under the federal Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act, a maker or dealer can't void your warranty just because you modified the car or used aftermarket parts. To deny a specific repair, they must show your change caused that failure, and the rest of the warranty stays in force.

Switching off connectivity in settings or the app is a feature the maker gives you, so it has no warranty effect at all.

Physical changes are where the risk sits, and it's narrow: if your hands-free mic or audio fails after you pulled a fuse or unplugged a module, expect that claim to be questioned. A transmission failure has nothing to do with a telematics fuse.

Keep yourself covered

  • Prefer reversible steps: antenna leads and fuses over cutting wires.
  • Keep the fuse or module, labelled, in the glovebox.
  • Write down what you changed and when, with photos.
  • If a claim is denied, ask for the reason in writing. The burden is on them to link it to your change.
  • Lease? Your contract may require returning the car as delivered. Check before modifying.

This is general information about US federal law, not legal advice. State lemon laws and your warranty booklet may add terms.

Checking a car in person takes two minutes

Before you buy

  • Look up. SOS, Assist or OnStar buttons on the mirror or overhead console almost always mean a modem.
  • Read the window sticker for the brand's service name: FordPass Connect, OnStar, Safety Connect, HondaLink, Blue Link, Kia Connect, NissanConnect Services, Starlink Safety and Security, Mazda Connected Services, Uconnect or SiriusXM Guardian, Car-Net, ConnectedDrive.
  • Open the settings. A connectivity, modem or data-sharing menu means hardware is there. On Fords, an "Onboard Modem Serial Number" entry means a modem that was never activated.
  • Ask the dealer to check the VIN's build sheet for a telematics unit.

After you buy

  • Remove previous owners. In the 2025 Subaru research, attackers could add themselves to a car's account without the owner being notified. Reset connectivity so old app accounts lose access (Ford: Connectivity Reset → Erase network data).
  • Opt out of every data-sharing toggle, then decide on hardware steps.
  • Pair carefully. Infotainment units store synced contacts and messages locally. Decline contact and message sync if you only need audio.
  • See what insurers hold. You can request your consumer file from LexisNexis Risk Solutions, one of the brokers GM sold driving data to.

Before you sell

  • Factory-reset the infotainment system to clear phones, contacts, addresses and garage codes.
  • Remove the car from the maker's app and cancel any subscription.
  • Remember a car with no modem still keeps data on board: crash event recorders and infotainment logs can be read by anyone with physical access and the right tools.

What's coming

From model year 2027, a US Commerce Department rule bars connected-vehicle software tied to China or Russia, and from model year 2030 their connectivity hardware. It limits who builds the modem, not whether cars have one.
